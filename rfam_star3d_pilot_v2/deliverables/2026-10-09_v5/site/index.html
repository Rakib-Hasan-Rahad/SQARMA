1. Overview

Research question

For distinct homologous RNA sequences with experimental structures, where do the correspondences in the ordinary Rfam seed alignment differ from those of the structural aligner original STAR3D, and what evidence explains the differences? Neither method is treated as ground truth.

Method in one sentence

For each selected RNA pair, we use one forward alignment from original STAR3D v1.2 with default parameters, following the package’s preprocessing procedure.

Dataset policy

The active analysis includes only experimental structures of verified natural RNA sequences. Confirmed engineered constructs and unresolved cases are excluded from the accepted cohort.

Headline

Under the natural-sequence policy, only the preQ1-I riboswitch family (RF00522) has accepted pairs (counts below). In its row for PDB 7REX, the 3′ strand of helix P1 appears shifted by one position in the seed. This is a candidate for curator review, not a demonstrated error: both supporting comparisons share the 7REX row, and in loop L1 the seed is better supported than STAR3D. All other previously analysed pairs used at least one engineered construct and are excluded; their records are kept as dataset-selection evidence.

4. Case study: RF00522 row for 7REX

Supporting evidence

  • After superposition on residues where both methods agree, STAR3D partners are closer than seed partners for every differing residue of the P1 3′ strand (region evidence below).
  • FR3D finds the STAR3D-register Watson–Crick pairs in 7REX; for the seed-register pairs no FR3D annotation is detected.
  • The 7REX authors describe P1 as a canonical A-form helix with a G5–C18 pair (the STAR3D register; PMC8752633).
  • The RNAs involved (3FU2, 6VUI, 7REX) are verified natural sequences under the v5 policy.

Counterevidence and caveats

  • Loop L1: the seed is better supported there, including the pocket-ceiling residues described by the 7REX authors.
  • STAR3D uses base pairing to build its alignment, so interaction preservation is supporting, not independent, evidence.
  • Both comparisons share the 7REX row: one observation, not two.
  • A second preQ1 ligand in 7REX contacts L1 and residue 16 of the candidate strand.

Limitations

  • A single family has accepted pairs, and those pairs share RNAs, so they are not independent.
  • For these families the ordinary Rfam seed equals the 3D-curated alignment (identical records), so this is not a sequence-only versus 3D comparison.
  • STAR3D ran under x86-64 emulation. Earlier sessions also ran reverse-direction and replicate alignments; they were not used to select or combine the primary mapping (archived; earlier observations of forward/reverse differences remain documented limitations).
  • Original STAR3D’s base-pair selection is broader than its paper describes (documented in the v4 method audit, archived).
  • Region classifications are an AI agent’s review, not a human’s.

Glossary

Seed alignment
Rfam’s curated multiple sequence alignment for a family; residues in the same column correspond.
Correspondence
A claim that nucleotide i of one RNA plays the same role as nucleotide j of another.
STAR3D
A structural aligner that superimposes helical stacks of two 3D structures and aligns the loops between them.
FR3D, cWW, stacking
FR3D annotates base pairs (cWW = canonical cis Watson–Crick) and base stacking in a structure.
Verified natural
The analysed construct reproduces a documented natural RNA sequence (synthesis or transcription allowed); engineered or extended constructs are excluded even if the changes were masked.
Denominator
The count an outcome is divided by; each table states its own.
